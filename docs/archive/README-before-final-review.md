# Las Vegas

I am making a small multiplayer tavern where friends can arrive, recognise one another, and choose a table to play together. I started with a browser version of Las Vegas, then realised I wanted the experience around the game as well: entering a warm room, seeing who is there, and deciding where to join. The tavern is the shared meeting place; the game gives people something to do together.

[Enter the tavern](https://comp4020-final-shuyangyuzu-cmd.fly.dev/).

## What good means here

For me, good means that a newly invited friend can understand the space and join a game without someone explaining the interface. They should choose a nickname and character, enter the map, recognise a playable table, and see whether it has room. Returning to the bar should clearly differ from giving up a game seat. Refreshing should preserve their identity rather than turn them into another player.

I also want the room to feel welcoming. Dark wood, warm lighting, recognisable furniture and small characters should make it somewhere to spend time, rather than a form with a decorative picture. My reference is [Gather](https://www.gather.town/): making other people's presence visible through a shared space. I take that spatial idea into a casual game setting. Stardew Valley's interiors and Red Dead Redemption 2's saloons are visual and atmosphere references, not sources of copied assets. The current artwork is drawn in code.

Fairness matters as much as atmosphere. The server must decide turns, rolls, legal placements and payouts. One person's duplicate click must not change the game twice, and two tables must not share a game accidentally. I use the [official Las Vegas rulebook](https://www.ravensburger.org/spielanleitungen/ecm/Spielanleitungen/26938_Vegas_EN.pdf) as the rules reference. Online and Offline now play four rounds. I deliberately chose a random first starter instead of the rulebook's oldest-player rule; the starting marker then moves one seat clockwise each round. Optional neutral dice and Royale modules remain outside scope.

## Try the current version

On first visit, choose Log in, Sign up, or Continue as guest. A guest's character lives only in that browser, exactly as before. Signing up (username and password — no email; save the recovery codes shown at registration for password resets) can optionally keep your current guest character via a checkbox, or starts a brand-new one. Logging in on a second device signs the first one out automatically and keeps the same character, including an in-progress game turn; logging out — from the character panel's own Account section — revokes that browser's access too. Choose clothing colour, skin tone and a nickname, then enter. Walk using WASD, arrow keys, clicking the floor, or the on-screen directions; holding a direction continues movement. Click a dining chair or bar stool to walk over and sit; use Stand up to leave. Four dining tables are evenly spaced, and a walkable restroom is at the map edge. Online patrons cannot occupy the same tile.

The nine game tables include three Vegas tables, two Hold'em tables, and a dominoes table (each Online and Offline); the offline blackjack table seats you and three NPCs around a half-round felt, playing against the dealer with 500 virtual chips per sitting, and an Online blackjack table supports up to four human players against the dealer. The following paragraph describes Vegas. Click a game chair to choose its colour, now five per table (a purple fifth joined the original four) to match the official rulebook's own base-game player count. The first player at an empty online table becomes host; later players request admission and the host approves or declines. Pending requests do not occupy seats. Invite a friend through the room link. With two to five seated players, everyone must be ready before the host starts. The solo table lets its starter choose a total player count from two to five (default five: one human and four NPCs) before it starts immediately. Roll your remaining dice, choose one face, and place all dice showing it. Equal dice counts cancel at settlement; the remaining players receive bills in descending order.

After rounds 1–3, the host clicks Start round 2, 3 or 4 on the settlement screen. Dice reset to eight and winnings carry over. Unawarded notes return to the bottom of the same deck; each casino receives at least $50,000 again. You can see your own cumulative bank, while everyone else's stays hidden until the end. Individual round payouts remain visible. After round 4, the screen announces the winner and final standings: most money wins, then most banknotes; an exact tie shares the win.

Use the Say something input and Send to speak publicly. Other people in the tavern see your text in a wrapping bubble over your character for about eight seconds. These new messages are public, not private conversation; previous private-chat records are not broadcast.

As a guest, use separate browsers, profiles or devices to try different players — identity is stored in that browser, and clearing its storage or switching devices does not recover the same player. A logged-in account instead follows you: log in from any device to resume the same character, stamina, held items and in-progress games.

Click the counter to open the physical book: The Recipe Book, Meals, and Drinks. The Recipe Book (renamed from The Mixology Book when it grew to cover food as well as drinks) lets Discover combine house recipes with player-published recipes of either kind, searchable by name, ingredients, tags or creator, and filterable by Drink/Food alongside the existing source/alcohol/pantry filters. Logged-in players choose Drink or Food when publishing, upload a photo, write the recipe, preview and publish it, then edit or batch-delete their own posts under My Recipes; a drink states whether it contains alcohol, while food instead takes optional prep/cook times. Viewing a published recipe opens a two-page spread — a photo and introduction on the left, measures/steps/actions on the right, each page scrolling independently of the other — with a temporary, reading-only pinch/scroll zoom on the photo that never changes the author's own saved composition. The editor's own photo can be dragged to reposition and zoomed with +/−/scroll/pinch; that composition is what gets saved and reused everywhere the photo appears. Draft changes stay private until explicitly published. Pantry, Saved Recipes and Party Plan belong to your account; a plan stores a recipe snapshot so later author edits do not change your shopping measures. Choose 1–20 servings/glasses, copy the shopping list and tick purchased ingredients. You can explicitly import the previous browser-local pantry/plan once. House recipes keep their sources, illustrations and estimated-measure notes. [Community recipe details](docs/community-recipes.md) cover limits, photo storage, moderation and verification; [recipe/conversion notes](docs/mixology-sources.md) explain the house adaptations.

Order a meal or drink from the book's own Meals/Drinks pages — one of each at a time. Carry what you're holding around the tavern; a small tag floats over your head showing what you have. Sit at a dining table or bar stool (never a game chair) and use the overhead prompt to eat or drink: a roughly eight-second animation plays while movement, sitting, ordering, and joining a game are all paused. Finishing restores stamina — +70% for a meal, +30% for a drink, capped at 100%. Stamina is shown in a HUD chip beside "Your character" and only ever decays while you're actually online; it has no effect on any game's rules, rolls, or ability to move, and a low reading is only ever a dismissable suggestion to eat or drink. A real-time Sydney/Canberra wall clock is mounted on the map for reference.

## What I can check, and what I must judge

The tests cover game actions, readiness, identity checks, table membership, migrations, exclusive tile occupancy, speech validation and expiry, persistence-related behaviour, and server-side action logging. Direction-control tests cover release and cancellation. They include complete four-round games for every player count, NPC continuation, private winnings and simultaneous next-round requests, plus poker settlement and recipe scaling/list persistence. Dated results are recorded in PLAN.md. `CLAUDE.md` turns these promises into agent constraints. Whether the room is inviting and its controls understandable needs actual use: I rejected the earlier form-led interface despite passing tests, and later found that painted chairs did not match their clickable tiles. My feedback and the agent's automated checks are separate evidence. A physical-phone long-press check remains outstanding.

For Crit 9, shared positions, presence, table status and game progress travel through server-sent events, targeting updates within about a second without reload. Earlier agent measurements recorded roughly 50–220 ms propagation; these are measurements from the documented runs, not a guarantee for every connection. Restart epochs and per-channel revisions prevent old responses from replacing newer state. I chose host-approved admission so friends can control their table, with transaction-validated capacity rather than optimistic client reservations; [the decision record](ADR-last-seat.md) explains alternatives and costs.

For Crit 10, every player and tavern action a person can take — joining, moving, sitting, speaking, creating or joining a table, approving a request, rolling, placing, leaving, being handed to an NPC — now writes one structured line to the server's own log output, not only to its database, so a presenter can narrate who did what by reading `flyctl logs` alone, without clicking through their own browser. A rejected attempt (a forged session, acting out of turn, a non-host trying to approve or disband) is logged too, tagged separately from a genuine success, and a session secret never appears in any logged line. [`ADR-access-control.md`](ADR-access-control.md) explains how an anonymous browser identity is recognised and recovered, how a tavern character and a game player correlate, and what each player may see or change and why.

## Limits and next steps

An online Vegas host cannot yet add or remove NPC seats before starting. The Vegas NPC engine advances while its room has a live subscriber; an unwatched Vegas game pauses. There is no real-money play. I prefer a usable gathering place with one reliable game over several unfinished games.

The current online and solo Vegas board presents six connected casino
sectors around a hexagonal dice tray on a wooden tabletop, with actual
bills outside each casino and coloured dice/counts inside. Vegas tables
in the tavern are round with five evenly spaced chairs. Solo NPC rooms
omit invitations; online rooms retain them. Both use the four-round
base-game flow described above.

## Exit and return

Both Online and Offline games offer Exit game during play and after settlement. Offline means a personal game against computer opponents; this website still needs internet access. Exiting ends an Offline game immediately. In an Online game, the departing seat passes to computer control without losing its dice or winnings, and the next human becomes host if needed. The last human's exit closes the room.

Back to the bar reserves the seat for three minutes, including invitations opened without a tavern identity. An explicit return before the deadline cancels the departure; passive heartbeats do not. After three minutes, the same exit policy applies. Expiry is checked on live ticks and on the first request after an idle/restart period. Repeated departure signals do not restart the clock. A scroll beside the game title opens the rules, including the four-round flow, random first starter and the difference from Royale modules.

## Room voice calls — discontinued

Voice calls were a real attempt (WebRTC mesh audio for a seated Online Vegas table, with signaling, membership, invites, reconnection handling, and a diagnostics tool all built and unit/component-tested — see `public/voiceCall.js` and `spec/voiceClient.test.ts`/`spec/voice.test.ts`/`spec/voiceDepartures.test.ts`), but it never reached a reliability bar I'd call done: several real browser-to-browser sessions over my own regular Wi-Fi failed to connect or produced one-way/no audio, no TURN relay has ever been configured (so some network pairs can structurally never connect peer-to-peer at all), and the underlying negotiation had more than one real timing bug found only by actually trying it (see PLAN.md's full history of what was found and fixed, and what still wasn't enough). Rather than keep presenting an unreliable feature as if it worked, the phone-icon entry point and the incoming-call prompt are no longer shown anywhere in the real interface — a seated player has no way to start or receive a call from this UI. The code underneath is untouched and its own tests still pass; this is a visibility decision, not a claim that the feature is now fixed or will be revisited.


## Texas Hold’em

I added saloon-style no-limit Hold’em in both rows. Click a Hold’em chair: Offline starts a personal table with four NPCs; the first player at an empty Online fixture hosts a private table. Everyone starts with 500 play chips, with blinds 1/2 and 2–5 seats. Search an online friend's nickname from the table, then click Invite. Their screen shows Join and Decline. A recipient who is already playing another game must leave it before joining. New arrivals during a hand wait for the next deal. Only you see your pocket cards until showdown.

Five little characters now sit around a round felt table, with your own seat at the bottom. Cards and bets appear on the tabletop; opponents' private cards remain face down until an authorized showdown reveal. Empty seats remain visible in Online play.

Use Fold, Check/Call, Bet/Raise or All in when it is your turn. Side pots, equal-hand splits and short-all-in raising rights are server-checked. The host clicks Deal next hand after settlement. Leave table exits immediately; an all-in already committed stays eligible for its pot. Refresh keeps the current hand. Disconnection reserves the seat for three minutes; a 45-second action timeout checks when free or folds when facing a bet. After settlement, a human with zero chips automatically leaves the table. An Out of chips popup shows the hand result; click Return to the bar to dismiss it and return. It survives refresh until acknowledged. There is no in-place rebuy; deliberately joining again after acknowledging starts a fresh free 500-chip buy-in if the previous stack was zero. This is play money, with no purchase or prize.

This first poker table supports five players rather than claiming an exact RDR2 replica. Blinds and timing are our house rules. NPCs are basic opponents, and only one group can occupy the Online Hold’em fixture at once. Voice calls were never wired into poker in the first place, and are now discontinued everywhere (see "Room voice calls" above). [The poker decision record](ADR-poker.md) explains sources, privacy, invitations and tradeoffs.

## Dominoes

Two independent pairs can play All Fives at once on the same physical table — four chairs, two facing pairs, each a separate match. Click a chair: the first person at an empty Online pair hosts it, later arrivals request to join and the host approves or declines; Offline starts a private match against an NPC opponent instead. Only your own hand is ever sent to your browser — the other player's tiles and the remaining stock are never exposed. Leaving or being away for three minutes ends only that pair's own match; the other pair keeps playing unaffected. [The decision record](ADR-dominoes.md) explains the rule variant and chair layout.


## Blackjack

Offline seats you with three NPCs around a semicircle table; their risk styles differ and each hit/stand is shown in sequence. Online has four human seats against the house dealer. Click the Online table or open a friend’s invite link. Choose a wager, Ready, then the host presses Deal when everyone is ready. All player cards are face up; the dealer has one hidden card until reveal. Hit, Stand, Double and eligible Split/Insurance controls appear only when legal. Natural blackjack pays 3:2, other wins 1:1, and the dealer stands on soft and hard 17. Split aces receive one card each. These table choices are documented in [the Blackjack decision record](ADR-blackjack.md).

Online cards, turns, wagers and settlement are server-authoritative and persist in SQLite. A newcomer during a hand waits for the next deal. Back to the bar reserves your seat for three minutes; Leave table releases it immediately and stands any committed hands. A 45-second turn timeout stands (or declines insurance); the dealer reveals and draws one step at a time. Out-of-chips players receive a Return to the bar notice after settlement. Online starts with 500 play chips per new table; Offline chips are local to the sitting. No real money or prizes.
