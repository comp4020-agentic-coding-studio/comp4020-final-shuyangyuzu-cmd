# Crit 8 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was making my expectations concrete enough to challenge the agent's reports. At first I had a working Las Vegas implementation, but trying the entry flow exposed broken invitations, unclear navigation and missing ways to leave. The agent repeatedly reported successful checks while I still saw the old interface. I kept describing what I actually saw instead of treating the test count as acceptance.

The same issue appeared in the tavern pivot. I wanted a place to enter and explore; the first version looked like forms with a map added. Referring to Gather and specifying a floating character editor, clothing and skin choices, and a following camera gave the agent a clearer target. I could then recognise the improvement and request a smaller interaction correction: holding a direction button to keep walking. The advance came from connecting an idea to observable behaviour, not simply adding features.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to become a developer who can direct an agent while retaining responsibility for the experience. I do not need to write every line to notice that an invitation is unusable or a room feels wrong. I do need to distinguish automated verification, deployment evidence and my own acceptance.

This week also made me value corrections that survive the next change: migration tests, versioned assets and input-cancellation rules belong in the harness. I am pausing unfinished ordering rather than presenting it as complete. My next step is to judge the current multiplayer flows through use and make my definition of good specific enough to guide further work.
